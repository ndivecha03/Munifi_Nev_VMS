SF SIRP · sidewalk contracts · 2025
23% LBE goal, essentially unmet

San Francisco's Sidewalk Inspection and Repair Program (SIRP) runs roughly $35M per year in concrete repair contracts. DPW has a stated goal of routing 23% of that work to Local Business Enterprise firms — the small, certified contractors that live in the same neighborhoods as the broken sidewalks. The routing infrastructure to actually hit that goal doesn't exist.

The Jan 2025 rule nobody's using yet

A law went live in January 2025 to fix this.

Amendments to SF Administrative Code Chapter 14B now let DPW award any construction work order up to $600,000 directly to a CMD-certified Micro-LBE contractor — no competitive solicitation required. For a $50,000–$300,000 sidewalk panel replacement, the rule applies trivially.

The 10% LBE bid discount and SBA-LBE bump also went live in January. CMD Director Regina Chan's office maintains the certified-firms directory. The policy infrastructure is there. The operational layer to use it isn't.

The matrix, in eight components

Each weight earns its keep against Chapter 14B.

Chapter 14B doesn't tell DPW which LBE contractor to pick — it tells them the conditions under which they can pick one directly. Our matrix scores eight signals that together answer the chapter's implicit "responsibility determination" question with full transparency. Every weight maps to a specific clause of Chapter 14B or to the policy intent the chapter serves.

LBE set-aside eligibility

Binary. Active CMD Micro-LBE certification + active CSLB C-8 license + cert not expired + work order ≤ $600K.

Rule alignment: This IS Chapter 14B §14B.7's literal eligibility test. A contractor that fails any condition cannot legally receive a direct set-aside dispatch.

Demographic equity

Stackable by LBE tier. Micro-LBE +12 · Small-LBE +9 · SBA-LBE +6 · LBE +4. Cap at 15.

Rule alignment: Chapter 14B is the LBE-specific small-purchase rule — designed by name for the certifications this component rewards. Higher-tier LBEs are harder to certify and represent more economically disadvantaged firms.

5 18 / 100

Proximity

Quadratic decay. 0 mi = full points · 3 mi ≈ 8.4 pts · 7 mi ≈ 2 pts.

Rule alignment: Chapter 14B's responsibility determination requires the agency to verify capacity to perform. For physical concrete work, response distance is a direct capacity signal — local crews mobilize faster and know local conditions.

6 10 / 100

Reliability

Positive. Active CSLB C-8 license, no recent contract terminations. Each termination subtracts 2 pts.

Rule alignment: Chapter 14B's responsibility determination requires the agency to verify the contractor is in good standing. This component captures the license-status check the chapter mandates.

7 4 / 100

Baseline capability

Binary floor. Active CSLB C-8 OR CMD cert — confirms the contractor has a concrete-trade credential beyond self-attestation.

Rule alignment: Chapter 14B requires the procurement to match the contractor's license classification. This floor confirms at least one external signal of concrete-trade capability.

8 3 / 100

Capacity headroom

Inverse. Contractors with low recent contract dollar volume have room to take work without saturation.

Rule alignment: Chapter 14B's responsibility test implicitly requires assessing whether the contractor can absorb the work. Low recent workload = clear headroom.

What the memo contains

  • SF Department of Public Works letterhead — Sidewalk Inspection and Repair Program
  • Section 1 — Procurement Summary: work-order number, defect type, NAICS / NIGP codes, site address, supervisor district, priority, est. completion
  • Section 2 — Policy Authority: Chapter 14B §14B.7 citation, confirmation of active CMD Micro-LBE certification, $600K threshold check
  • Section 3 — Price Quotes: structured fields for the 3-quote rule (or unable-to-obtain explanation), with price-reasonableness determination
  • Section 4 — Selected Contractor: legal business name, address, CMD cert #, LBE tier, CSLB #, contact
  • Section 5 — Responsibility Determination Checklist: pre-filled attestations per §14B.7
  • Section 6 — Authorization: signature blocks for DPW contracting officer + SIRP program manager

Why this matters

A rule, finally operational.

San Francisco enacted Chapter 14B's Micro-LBE construction set-aside in January 2025. It has done essentially zero SIRP dispatches under it. Our tool exists to change three things at once:

Wealth, not just concrete. ~$6.1M per year of SIRP work that currently flows to large prime contractors could instead route to 80–150 LBE-certified concrete firms across SF's neighborhoods — at the same per-job cost, under existing law, with no new spending required.

Transparency, not friction. Every dispatch logs why a contractor was picked — score, components, LBE tier, distance, prior city work. Reviewers can replay any decision the system makes. DPW staff get auto-generated Chapter 14B justification memos in place of hours of paperwork.

Contractors discoverable, not invisible. Dozens of CMD-certified C-8 concrete firms hold zero direct city SIRP contracts. The matrix surfaces the Micro-LBE firms who are set-aside eligible, points the next tier toward eligibility, and turns the rest into a knowable, geocoded pool DPW can actually see.

Three changes. No new laws. Same complaints. Same budget. Different recipients of the dollars.

A framework, not a feature

Four more SF 311 backlogs. Same matrix.
